# Storyline: The Last Chai

## Fictional case ARG-2342

Professor Rao was developing the experimental ARGUS surveillance system in a fictional college research laboratory. Shortly after 23:42, Rao is found dead. A prototype device and mobile phone are missing. A chai cup remains at the scene. The dark CCTV still looks damaged, and ARGUS cannot reliably reconstruct the night.

The workshop participants are asked to recover the staged timestamp, compare two prepared scenes, isolate the red backpack, and track its movement through a controlled corridor sequence. Each operation teaches an OpenCV building block; none is presented as a complete forensic identification system.

## Suspects

- **Aarav**, research assistant: had research-area access, says he left before the incident, and owns the red backpack.
- **Kabir**, laboratory technician: had lab and surveillance equipment access, says he did not approach the exit after 23:40, and is the canonical fictional culprit.
- **Meera**, research student: worked on a separate experiment and reports seeing someone with a bag.
- **Ramesh**, security guard: was assigned to the corridor and says nobody suspicious passed the exit; this account conflicts with the staged sequence.

Initials-based, generated portraits are decorative fictional representations, never evidence of identity.

## Evidence progression

1. CLAHE and a clock crop make the prepared 23:42 overlay easier to read. That is an image-processing exercise, not proof of time of death.
2. A pixel-difference operation compares the staged scenes. The prepared inventory says the phone and prototype are missing and an extra cup appears later. Optional COCO YOLO inference may report only classes it actually detects; it cannot recognize the invented prototype.
3. HSV segmentation marks red candidates. The fictional case record establishes Aarav's ownership; color does not.
4. The tracker observes a synthetic red candidate crossing a line. The narrative record identifies Kabir as carrier. A separately prepared ARGUS log reports 23:44.

The accusation is supported by the combination of visual movement, case-file statements and a fictional system log. The red backpack alone does not prove guilt.
